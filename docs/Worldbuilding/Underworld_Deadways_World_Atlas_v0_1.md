# UNDERWORLD — The Realms of the Deadways

**World atlas & visual direction — v0.1**  
**Status:** Exploratory setting proposal, **not established canon**  
**Date:** 7 November 2026  
**Project:** *Underworld*, a level-based cyberundead action-platformer  
**Available concept:** [Railway-city painting](Underverdenens%20majest%C3%A6tiske%20jernbaneby.png)  
**Referenced regional set:** `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/` — not present in this checkout (reviewed 7 November 2026)  
**Project alignment:** [Document responsibilities, open decisions, and asset inventory](README.md)

> **Worldbuilding north star:** The underworld is not a gothic imitation of the living world. It is an immense, alien world upon which the dead have built their own civilization.

## 1. Core vision

The underworld is a vast and ancient landscape whose physical and ecological processes need not resemble those of the living world. Spectral rivers cross its basins, chasms, wilderness, and impossible horizons. Cities cling to their banks. Railways traverse the rivers and reach outward into regions where even the dead struggle to comprehend the terrain.

The familiar elements—the stations, street markets, cafés, apartments, cathedrals, factories, bridges, and timetables—are the **work of the dead**. They are deliberate attempts to build recognizable places within an otherwise strange reality. Their inhabitants have ordinary lives, extraordinary customs, and concerns unrelated to the protagonist's journey.

The unfamiliar elements—the vast translucent rib-like arches, landscapes that shift or unfold, alien organisms, inverted spaces, otherworldly reflections, and unidentifiable structures—belong to the world itself. They should feel **consistent enough to inhabit but never entirely domesticated**.

The aesthetic is **cyber-gothic afterlife meets alien natural history**: charcoal and black stone; aged bronze, rail steel, and warm interior light; cyan spectral rivers; restrained magenta as an expressive accent; impossible geological and biological forms. Colour and mood vary substantially by region.

### The central contrast

- **Familiarity belongs to civilization.** The dead preserve, repair, reinterpret, and reinvent habits they remember from life.
- **Alienness belongs to the environment.** The world's natural laws do not exist to reassure its inhabitants.
- **Both can be beautiful, funny, sad, or frightening.** The most alien place need not be the most dangerous; the most human city need not be the safest.
- **The player sees a real society, not scenery arranged for a quest.** Trains have other passengers, settlements have local disputes, and creatures have behaviours independent of combat.

## 2. Foundational geography

### The Rivers

The great spectral rivers are among the oldest known features of the underworld. They form currents, confluences, basins, falls, tides, and hazardous crossings, although they may not be ordinary water. Different rivers may exhibit different behaviours and associations: memory, passage, loss, change, or separation. Those associations should arise through the cultures inhabiting their banks, not necessarily through simplistic literal magical effects.

**Unresolved:** Whether the rivers bring newly dead people *into* the world, or merely transport inhabitants *through* it.

### The Deadways

The Deadways are railway arteries built by the dead to follow, bridge, or circumvent the rivers. They are the technological successors to the mythic image of ferries and crossings, particularly the Styx: **the rivers are primordial, the railways historical**.

Grand terminals grow at river and rail junctions; wayside stations serve remote settlements; forgotten branches disappear into uncharted territories. Different lines may have different operators, histories, and standards. A timetable can be both an engineering achievement and a magnificent work of fiction.

### The Hinterlands

Beyond the rail corridors lie territories whose ecologies and geometries are poorly understood. They are not necessarily uninhabited: they may contain small settlements, nomadic communities, and societies entirely absent from familiar maps. Some routes remain stable for centuries, while others change slowly or respond to environmental events.

**Geographic principle:** The eight realms below are regions and environmental systems, **not** eight stacked floors, a fixed sequence of levels, or eight culturally homogeneous nations. Lines branch, reconnect, and become lost.

## 3. Regional atlas

### I. The Confluence — City of a Thousand Departures

**Identity:** The inhabited heart; junction city; possible starting region.  
**Mood:** Busy, luminous, melancholy, occasionally absurd.  
**Visual palette:** Cyan rivers, magenta cathedral windows, golden lamps, charcoal masonry.

Where several great spectral rivers meet, a vast city has accumulated over centuries. Cathedral-like towers share foundations with cramped tenements, railway offices, mausoleum conversions, suspended markets, and giant stations. Viaducts crisscross ravines; inhabited terraces climb the cliffs. Its districts reflect many eras and communities rather than one architectural or political tradition.

The rivers may interweave without mixing. Beyond the buildings stand translucent structures resembling the bones of impossible creatures. The Last Platform—a familiar station of delayed departures, lonely benches, and a skeletal cat—belongs here, or on the edge of this region.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Rooftop momentum, rail yards, alleys, vertical streets, hidden apartment passages, crowded station infrastructure.  
**Secrets:** Lost tickets, small memorials, private gardens, sealed flats, branch platforms, inconspicuous personal stories.  
**Narrative function:** Introduce the everyday humanity of the dead before showing the immensity of their environment.

### II. The Drowned Wards — Beneath the Riverline

**Identity:** Older city swallowed by spectral waters.  
**Mood:** Quietly occupied, reflective, submerged, haunted by history.  
**Visual palette:** Turquoise depths, wet slate, faded magenta glass, amber stilt-village lights.

Below the newer districts lie abandoned streets, drowned cathedrals, and half-functional underground railways. Upper floors and rooftops remain inhabited; residents navigate by boats, elevated walkways, and improvised platforms. Enormous pumping stations still operate, although different communities disagree about whether they are holding back water or restraining something below.

The reflective water does not always show the city above. Tides may follow distant processes rather than a visible moon. The Ward's inhabitants have learned to live with water that can be both infrastructure and mystery.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Sluices, barges, narrow dry ledges, flooded passages, pump machinery, vertiginous crossings.  
**Secrets:** Submerged archives, inaccessible upper floors, receding-tide passages, artifacts carried by currents.  
**Narrative function:** Show that civilization survives through adaptation, not mastery.

**Art reference (pending in this checkout):** 02 — The Drowned Wards — `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/02_The_Drowned_Wards.png`

### III. The Bellworks — Industry at the Edge of Eternity

**Identity:** Industrial heartland, rail yards, engineering districts.  
**Mood:** Industrious, overwhelming, noisy, stubbornly functional.  
**Visual palette:** Furnace amber, oxidized metal, electric cyan, smoky violet.

Great foundries, switching yards, turbines, cranes, and machine halls rise like funerary cathedrals beside the rivers. Spectral currents drive much of the region's infrastructure. Some machines are ancient and repaired without full understanding of their original purpose. Workers still maintain shifts, argue over equipment, and keep public services running.

Larger mechanisms may affect the surrounding landscape. Not all technology is comprehensible, and not every mysterious mechanism is malicious.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Moving trains, conveyor platforms, gear hazards, machinery puzzles, projectile ricochets, dense industrial encounters.  
**Secrets:** Forgotten maintenance shafts, dormant production lines, workers' quarters, unusually operated machinery.  
**Narrative function:** Show the ingenuity and compromises of the dead, and how much of the world depends on their labour.

**Art reference (pending in this checkout):** 03 — The Bellworks — `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/03_The_Bellworks.png`

### IV. The Pale Gardens — Where the Dead Things Grow

**Identity:** Alien ecology; living architecture; remote settlements.  
**Mood:** Wonder, tranquility, delicate unease.  
**Visual palette:** Soft cyan, translucent pearl, violet, luminous rose, dark teal.

Outside industrial corridors, the Deadways run through forests of luminous, jointed structures, enormous flowers, suspended membranes, and slow-growing organisms with no straightforward analogue in the living world. Some respond to sounds, vibration, heat, or the passage of trains. Settlements cultivate some species and adapt their homes to others.

The gardens should not simply be a safe version of an enchanted forest. Their ecology is genuinely alien, but sometimes peaceful. A player may find beauty where they expected danger.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Springing growths, towering natural climbs, sound-responsive platforms, moving organic structures, unusual wildlife.  
**Secrets:** Living hollows, settlements hidden inside growths, changes triggered by environmental rhythms, secluded gardens.  
**Narrative function:** Establish that the world is more than necropolises and machines.

**Art reference (pending in this checkout):** 04 — The Pale Gardens — `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/04_The_Pale_Gardens.png`

### V. The Ossuary Expanse — The Bones of Impossible Things

**Identity:** Colossal wilds, archaic structures, nomadic life.  
**Mood:** Awe, exposure, deep time, loneliness.  
**Visual palette:** Pale bone, blue-white translucency, violet distance, sparse warm encampments.

Rib-like arches span entire valleys; vertebral formations rise into mountain ranges; enormous cavities shelter villages and stations. These may be fossils, geological systems, or something neither. Some structures glow from within or appear to move over immense stretches of time.

Rail engineers use the formations as natural supports. Nomads and scavengers live among the structures, their settlements tiny against the landscape.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Vast climbs, exposed bridges, long falls, wind-blown plateaus, scavenger encounters, dramatic scale shifts.  
**Secrets:** Cavities larger inside than outside, ancient camps, enigmatic sites, paths exposed by environmental movement.  
**Narrative function:** Remind the player how small the known world is.

**Art reference (pending in this checkout):** 05 — The Ossuary Expanse — `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/05_The_Ossuary_Expanse.png`

### VI. The Inverted Reach — Where the World Forgets Which Way Is Down

**Identity:** Suspended cities, opposing surfaces, unusual gravity.  
**Mood:** Dizzying, sublime, oddly domestic within the impossible.  
**Visual palette:** Cyan waterfalls, cloud white, indigo void, magenta windows.

Entire valleys and cities face one another across a vast open space. Rivers travel across both orientations and sometimes fall between them. Railway lines cross bridges that appear to connect opposing landscapes. Residents have evolved everyday conventions for describing location, orientation, and travel.

Any gravity transitions must be local, legible, and learnable—not arbitrary disturbances to arcade controls.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Inverted traversal, orientation-specific routes, suspended carriages, vertical action, bridges crossing impossible voids.  
**Secrets:** Overlapping routes on opposite surfaces, unusual vantage points, hidden neighbourhoods between orientations.  
**Narrative function:** Challenge assumptions about space without destroying physical consistency.

**Art reference (pending in this checkout):** 06 — The Inverted Reach — `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/06_The_Inverted_Reach.png`

### VII. The Mirror Tides — An Ocean That Remembers Somewhere Else

**Identity:** Distant coast, black mirror ocean, uncertain identity and memory.  
**Mood:** Stillness, longing, ambiguity, quiet wonder.  
**Visual palette:** Black glass, starlight blue, isolated rose and amber reflections.

Some rivers terminate in a vast, still sea whose reflections show other places rather than what stands overhead: unfamiliar skies, lost cities, alternate landscapes, perhaps memories. Coastal settlements include observatories, fishing villages, and people waiting for particular images to return. Rail causeways extend toward remote islands, and some shores seem deeper than their geography permits.

Do not prematurely decide what the reflections truly are. Their inhabitants may have incompatible interpretations.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Coastal ruins, causeways, moving boats, reflected routes, apparitions, observation-based secrets.  
**Secrets:** Hidden islands, failed expeditions, unrecorded shorelines, paths revealed only by the reflected world.  
**Narrative function:** Offer a setting for questions of love, memory, identity, and whether an image of someone is the same as their continued existence.

**Art reference (pending in this checkout):** 07 — Mirror Tides panel study — `Underworld_Deadways_Zone_Concepts/07_The_Mirror_Tides_panel_study.png`

### VIII. The Uncharted Verge — Beyond the Last Known Line

**Identity:** Unmapped territory beyond the dependable rail network.  
**Mood:** Unsettling freedom, mystery, possibility, grandeur.  
**Visual palette:** Vast violet horizons, strange cyan structures, sparse magenta signals.

Here, track fragments end and rivers stop behaving like familiar routes. Landmasses unfold or shift, immense translucent forms cross distant spaces, and settlements or artifacts suggest histories outside every known map. The Verge is not automatically the final level; its narrative place depends on the protagonist's journey.

Its strangeness should be patterned, not a license for arbitrary rules. The dead may simply know less about this place than elsewhere.

**Gameplay possibilities:** Isolated stages, fragments of abandoned track, unique local traversal rules, mysterious encounters and discoveries.  
**Secrets:** Lost civilizations, off-map stations, routes once thought impossible, encounters that overturn earlier assumptions.  
**Narrative function:** Keep the world larger than the story we are presently telling.

## 4. Travel, stage structure, and exploration

Underworld remains a **fast, discrete, replayable stage-based arcade platformer**, not a mandatory backtracking Metroidvania. The Deadways give the player a coherent reason to move between regions and to find optional branches. Not every stage must begin or end with a train; the rail network serves as geography, recurring imagery, transportation, and narrative connective tissue.

- **Main routes:** Connect major hubs and ordinary stage progression.
- **Branch routes:** Lead to optional stages, peculiar communities, secrets, or alternate perspectives on familiar geography.
- **Lost stations:** Reward observation and movement mastery with locations that feel like parts of the world rather than disconnected bonus rooms.
- **Regional laws:** Give selected environments distinct interactions, with clear anticipation, readable rules, and reliable player controls.
- **Civilized versus alien:** Provide contrast within stages. A familiar platform may overlook something incomprehensible; the wilderness may shelter a peaceful village.

### Collectibles — open design direction

The game already distinguishes immediate ectoplasm ammunition from permanent weapon capabilities. Further collectibles should have **different purposes**, not become redundant currencies:

1. **Run resources:** Ectoplasm from combat, corpses, and caches; health restoration where appropriate.
2. **Mechanical secrets:** Weapon-specific modifiers, optional challenges, and discoveries that extend tactical choice without XP grinding.
3. **World keepsakes:** Letters, relics, personal possessions, recorded histories, surprising encounters, and evidence of life among the dead. *Keepsakes* is a provisional category name.

Secrets should be signalled by the environment and reward attention, movement skill, unusual weapon use, or curiosity—not dependence on firing at every wall. Some world secrets can simply be beautiful, funny, or sad. Optional exploration should enrich the journey without holding the main ending hostage.

## 5. Inhabitants, ecology, and conflict

**Undeath is a condition, not a moral category.** A skeleton may be a railway worker, a dangerous raider, a traveller, or a neighbour. A ghost may be a person, an echo, or something not yet understood; these possibilities are not automatically interchangeable. The protagonist, too, is undead.

Design communities with independent livelihoods: railway operators, maintenance crews, historians, gardeners, merchants, pilgrims, nomads, scavengers, and people who simply want to be left alone. Religions and cultures may interpret the world differently without any one of them being presented as the definitive explanation.

Alien creatures need observable ecological behaviours beyond attacking the player. Some are attracted to trains; others respond to spectral rivers, shifting light, or unfamiliar phenomena. The environment should not be automatically hostile simply because it is strange.

**Unresolved metaphysical question:** What actually happens when an undead body is defeated, and why can necromancers reactivate some of those remains? Mechanical combat rules exist, but the setting's deeper answer remains open.

## 6. The protagonist's journey

A small undead traveller, identified visually by their vivid magenta soul-flame, moves through this immense landscape seeking their soulmate. Their relationship, the circumstances of the separation, the location of the soulmate, and the journey's ending are **not yet defined**.

This is the emotional anchor of the world: the terrain grows increasingly impossible while the traveller's purpose remains intensely personal. The soulmate should ultimately be a person rather than a victory prize. Recurring motifs, places, objects, or music may make the absent person felt throughout the adventure without revealing the story prematurely.

Bosses and permanent weapon rewards should emerge from the regions and their inhabitants. A major opponent may be defending a home, maintaining a duty, acting on a misunderstanding, or pursuing something unrelated to the protagonist. Winning need not mean killing.

## 7. Possible outer continuity: Blacklight (nonbinding)

The world **may** belong to a Live Mausoleum simulation within the larger *Blacklight* universe. This would be an optional creator-level explanation of the world's origin, **not** an obligatory twist, a required reference, or a constraint on the internal story. Its inhabitants' relationships and experiences remain meaningful on their own terms.

No current decision establishes whether the world is simulated, whether every inhabitant is a hosted continuation, or who designed its physics. The game must work as a self-contained setting without this connection.

## 8. Visual development notes

The supplied railway-city painting and the referenced regional image set are **concept art, not gameplay-ready parallax/background layers**. It emphasizes regional silhouettes, colour, atmosphere, and civilization amidst alien scale. Translating it into playable levels will require clear foreground silhouettes, traversable geometry, visual hierarchy, readable hazards, and separate background layers.

**Shared motifs:** Spectral cyan rivers, Deadway rails, monumental crossings, warm islands of habitation, magenta soul-flame, unexpected little acts of ordinary life, and geometry that suggests older nonhuman processes.

**Avoid:** Making every zone the same neon-gothic city; presenting religious afterlives as one-to-one game regions; replacing mystery with arbitrary physics; treating everyone undead as evil; and mistaking generated concept images for established lore.

## 9. Questions to investigate before committing canon

- Do spectral rivers deliver the newly dead, or carry existing inhabitants between regions?
- What, physically or metaphysically, is an undead person in this world?
- Can the dead die again, and how does corpse reactivation work in fiction?
- Are the Deadways governed by a shared authority or competing regional operators?
- Which alien phenomena are stable laws of nature, and which are mysteries or local exceptions?
- How long have civilizations occupied the world? What predates them?
- How does the protagonist know where to search? What exactly can be lost and found?
- What is the final emotional destination of the journey, independent of its geography?
- Which region concepts survive playtesting as genuinely distinctive stage experiences?
