# THE ICONS OF UNDERWORLD
*Notable artists, celebrities, fashion pioneers and public figures of the Deadways*

---

## 01. Mara Vell — The White Widow
**Occupation:** Fashion icon, performer, creative director  
**Associated with:** Vesper Form, Velvet & Bone  
**Cultural movement:** Eternal Mourning

Mara Vell is among the most recognizable figures in Confluence fashion. A skeletal woman known for her immaculate ivory appearance, elaborate mourning veils, and theatrical black-and-white ensembles, she transformed traditional funerary dress into a language of luxury and seduction.

Her most famous collection, *The Funeral That Never Happened*, consisted of ceremonial gowns designed for people mourning versions of themselves they had abandoned. The collection made her a household name and popularized the extravagant modern mourning aesthetic.

Despite her title, Mara has never publicly identified the person she supposedly mourns. She once remarked that she was "widowed by my own death," but has offered several contradictory explanations since.

Her association with Vesper Form made her fabulously successful. Critics accuse her of turning self-acceptance into an expensive commodity, particularly after the company began marketing premium reconstruction services using her image.

Her defenders point out that she has commissioned independent designers and funded exhibitions that major galleries refused to host.

**Cultural legacy:** Ivory skeletal ornamentation, dramatic mourning collars, black veils worn as everyday fashion, and ceremonial clothing designed to celebrate transformation.

**Public reputation:** Brilliant, vain, generous when she chooses to be, and extraordinarily difficult to impress.

## 02. Rook Ravel — The Cracked Saint
**Occupation:** Street artist, fashion provocateur, former industrial repair worker  
**Associated with:** Gravepunk, independent bodycraft workshops  
**Cultural movement:** The Unrestored

Rook Ravel became famous after appearing at a prestigious fashion exhibition wearing an old industrial uniform, a cracked funerary mask, and a jacket covered in visible repair stitching.

It was an intentional insult to the prevailing fashion for flawless bodily reconstruction.

His subsequent performances made damaged and repaired anatomy a source of aesthetic pride. Instead of concealing scars and imperfections, he incorporated them into his clothing and public image.

Rook's most famous work, *The Cost of Being Whole*, was a collection of reconstructed garments displayed alongside the prices charged for the repairs their owners had needed.

The exhibition was closed after several sponsors withdrew their support.

Today, his cracked-halo symbol appears on clothing, railway pillars, walls and occasionally directly on bodies. Some consider him a visionary; others see an abrasive opportunist who has turned working-class hardship into fashionable rebellion.

His greatest embarrassment came when luxury boutiques began selling artificially distressed garments inspired by his work.

He responded by selling a single pristine white shirt at an outrageous price, labeling it *The Revolution, Retail Edition*.

It sold immediately.

**Cultural legacy:** Visible repairs, industrial salvage fashion, cracked-halo imagery, and the idea that deterioration can be beautiful rather than shameful.

**Public reputation:** Charismatic, confrontational, funny, and suspicious of anyone claiming to speak for the poor.

## 03. Edda Static — The Voice Beneath the Rails
**Occupation:** Singer, composer, producer  
**Associated with:** The Blue Note, The Last Record  
**Cultural movement:** Cathedral Noise

Edda Static is credited with popularizing one of the Confluence's most distinctive musical movements.

Growing up around the industrial districts of the Bellworks, she became fascinated by the sounds of machinery: railway signals, resonating bridge supports, damaged speakers, electric discharge, and the repetitive rhythms of repair workshops.

Her early compositions incorporated industrial recordings into brutally distorted electronic music. Over time, she developed a style combining intricate melodic hooks, harsh percussion, spectral choral harmonies and intimate, almost whispered vocals.

Her breakthrough performance took place beneath a railway viaduct, where passing trains became an unpredictable part of the arrangement.

The recording, *No Silence Below*, made her famous.

Edda's public image is striking: a tall undead woman in a ragged formal coat, her face partially concealed by a funerary sound mask adorned with tiny resonating plates. Her performances use carefully controlled vocal processing, making her voice sound like several damaged recordings attempting to sing in unison.

Her conflict with The Last Record over ownership of early performances became a celebrated dispute over whether centuries-long commercial contracts should outlive an artist's willingness to honor them.

She maintains that imperfect sounds possess emotional qualities that pristine instruments often erase.

**Cultural legacy:** Industrial tracker music, distorted vocal performance, bridge acoustics, and the popularization of intentionally broken electronic sound.

**Public reputation:** Brilliant, intense, privately reserved, and notoriously protective of younger musicians.

## 04. Corven Rusk — The Golden Anatomist
**Occupation:** Bodycraft designer, sculptor, luxury entrepreneur  
**Associated with:** Vesper Form, private galleries  
**Cultural movement:** Anatomical Baroque

Corven Rusk believes that the undead body is an unfinished work of art.

His creations combine skeletal anatomy with elaborate gilded fittings, sculptural collars, ornamental prosthetics and funerary geometry. His most celebrated designs resemble walking reliquaries: magnificent, unsettling, and deliberately artificial.

He rose to prominence by rejecting the idea that reconstructed bodies should imitate living anatomy.

"Why pretend to have flesh," he famously asked, "when bone can be made into architecture?"

His clientele includes wealthy collectors, performers, celebrities and members of the Confluence's most influential social circles.

Rusk's work is genuinely innovative. Several techniques first explored in his artistic commissions have influenced less expensive practical bodycraft.

Nevertheless, his business practices have attracted criticism. He treats exclusivity as an artistic principle, sometimes declining commissions from customers he considers insufficiently interesting.

His admirers describe him as the greatest sculptor of his generation.

His detractors call him a merchant of beautiful inequality.

He regards both descriptions as compliments.

**Cultural legacy:** Gilded skeletal ornamentation, architectural body silhouettes, ceremonial prosthetic design, and the treatment of anatomy as sculpture.

**Public reputation:** Cultivated, arrogant, aesthetically fearless, and commercially formidable.

## 05. Tavi Senn — The Last Mile
**Occupation:** Competitive runner, stunt performer, media personality  
**Associated with:** Crosswake Lines, Afterimage Media  
**Cultural movement:** Deadway racing culture

Tavi Senn became a celebrity by doing something that railway authorities repeatedly warned people not to do: racing along the city's most dangerous elevated transit structures.

A wiry ghoul with a distinctive asymmetrical stride and a passion for spectacular entrances, Tavi turned informal industrial obstacle races into popular public entertainment.

Her performances combine athletic movement, dangerous vertical traversal, rooftop sprints and carefully choreographed leaps between railway structures.

To her admirers, she embodies the thrill of refusing to accept physical limitations.

To transport workers, she is an ongoing maintenance and safety nightmare.

Crosswake Lines eventually sponsored her, transforming an infamous trespasser into the public face of a profitable racing circuit.

The resulting competitions are enormously popular, particularly among younger residents.

But the professionalization of the sport has caused conflict. Entry fees are rising, independent racers have difficulty obtaining access, and specialized equipment has become increasingly expensive.

Tavi publicly insists that the sport must remain accessible to anyone willing to take the risk.

Her sponsors are less enthusiastic about that position.

**Cultural legacy:** Racing jackets, flamboyant sprint trails, branded athletic footwear, rooftop sporting culture, and competitive movement as public spectacle.

**Public reputation:** Reckless, charming, fiercely competitive, and more thoughtful than her advertising suggests.

## 06. Iona Fen — The Keeper of the Unclaimed
**Occupation:** Installation artist, independent archivist  
**Associated with:** Public memorial movements, neighborhood collectives  
**Cultural movement:** Civic Remembrance

Iona Fen is an artist whose work occupies the uncomfortable boundary between memorial, historical archive and political protest.

Originally known for intricate sculptures made from discarded personal possessions, she became famous for *The Unclaimed*, a sprawling installation beneath a major Confluence viaduct.

The work uses thousands of individually illuminated elements to represent people missing from surviving municipal records.

Unlike conventional funerary monuments, it contains no idealized portraits and no heroic inscriptions.

Only absence.

The installation became a place where families and friends could gather, leave messages, and dispute official accounts of disappearances.

Its popularity also created significant controversy.

Municipal authorities questioned the accuracy of some entries. Property owners objected to its placement. Critics accused Iona of turning private suffering into a public spectacle.

Iona has responded by making the work's methods, corrections and unresolved disputes visible to its visitors.

She refuses to claim that every missing person is dead.

Her quiet insistence on that distinction has become central to her reputation.

**Cultural legacy:** Participatory memorial installations, unofficial civic archives, remembrance through absence rather than idealized portraiture.

**Public reputation:** Principled, meticulous, emotionally guarded, and frustratingly unwilling to simplify an argument for television.

## 07. Nix Corven — The Laughing Obituary
**Occupation:** Broadcaster, comedian, satirist  
**Associated with:** Afterimage Media, independent political theater  
**Cultural movement:** Mortuary Satire

Nix Corven is the most famous satirist in the Confluence—and, according to his own advertisements, the most frequently sued.

An elegantly dressed skeleton with a permanently mischievous stage persona, Nix hosts *Still Here*, a popular broadcast combining interviews, political commentary, absurdist comedy and elaborate mock funeral ceremonies for public figures whose careers have collapsed.

His performances frequently involve a ceremonial coffin.

At the beginning of each show, Nix reads the name of something he believes should have died: a disastrous public policy, an expired fashion trend, an obsolete institution, or a particularly offensive advertisement.

He then delivers its eulogy.

His audience includes railway workers, artists, affluent residents and political officials who insist they never watch him.

Nix's greatest talent is making complex public disputes understandable through comedy. His greatest weakness is that he sometimes prefers a devastating joke to a fair account of its subject.

Afterimage Media profits enormously from his controversies while periodically threatening to cancel his program.

He has announced his retirement seventeen times.

Nobody believes him anymore.

**Cultural legacy:** Mock funerals as satire, black-humor broadcasting, irreverent political theater and a vast library of insults that have entered common speech.

**Public reputation:** Hilarious, exhausting, perceptive, occasionally cruel, and almost impossible to embarrass.

## 08. Sable Aster — The Woman Beyond the Last Line
**Occupation:** Explorer, photographer, travel writer  
**Associated with:** Ninth Signal, independent expeditions, travel publications  
**Cultural movement:** The New Sublime

Sable Aster became famous for documenting landscapes beyond the dependable Deadway network.

Her work depicts settlements beneath impossible skeletal formations, abandoned railway stations, strange rivers, and enormous natural structures whose origins remain unexplained.

Her most influential publication, *Where the Tracks Forget*, transformed the aesthetic of underworld exploration.

Where earlier commercial travel publications emphasized safety, comfort and familiar destinations, Sable portrayed the unknown as magnificent, unsettling and worthy of serious attention.

She is often depicted wearing a dark expedition coat adorned with old travel emblems, a battered funerary veil, and a collection of instruments accumulated over many journeys.

Her photographs and recorded expeditions have made remote regions fashionable among wealthy travelers.

That success troubles her.

Some communities she documented have since experienced aggressive commercial development and intrusive tourism.

Sable now refuses to publish the locations of certain settlements and natural sites, prompting accusations that she is deliberately withholding valuable geographical information.

She argues that discovering something does not automatically grant the right to sell it.

**Cultural legacy:** Expedition fashion, alien landscape photography, interest in forgotten railway routes, and a movement toward treating the underworld's natural environment as worthy of wonder rather than conquest.

**Public reputation:** Adventurous, enigmatic, stubborn, and increasingly hostile to the tourism industry that helped make her famous.
