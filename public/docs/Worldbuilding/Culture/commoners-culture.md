Oh, I think we should give them the same attention we gave Mara Vell, Edda Static, and the other stars. ❤️

Because these six people are perhaps even more important for making the Confluence believable.

The celebrities show us what the culture *aspires to be*. Ordinary citizens show us what it actually *is*.

And I don't want them to become six representatives of six social classes. Each should be someone with a life, ambitions, contradictions, little vanities, and a history that doesn't revolve around Cole.

Let's treat the six people we sketched as our first **Confluence Citizen Profiles**. Their occupations, appearances, and basic personalities come from our previous exploration. I'll deepen their characterization here, while keeping the additions provisional.

# **UNDERWORLD — Citizens of the Confluence**

Population study · Character biographies and visual direction · Volume I

## **01. Vera — The Station Seamstress**

Skeleton · Tailor · Independent artisan · Lower station district

**Appearance**

Vera is a narrow-framed skeletal woman with aged, slightly darkened ivory bones. Her skull is delicate but not conventionally beautiful: a small asymmetry in her jaw gives her face a permanently skeptical expression.

She wears a practical plum-colored dress beneath a short black work coat. A modest mourning veil hangs from a metal comb attached to the back of her skull, its fine mesh carefully repaired in several places.

Her clothing borrows from Mara Vell's theatrical mourning aesthetic, but everything has been adapted for work.

Decorative silver rings attached to her skeletal fingers double as measuring and sewing implements. Her fitted belt holds cutting tools, folded patterns, and small compartments of thread.

A pair of tiny cyan lenses can be lowered over her eye sockets when she's doing detailed work.

Her entire appearance is carefully maintained, but nothing is new.

**The person beneath the clothes**

Vera runs a modest alterations workshop near one of the older stations. Her clients range from commuters needing cheap repairs to performers seeking complicated custom garments.

She is proud of her craft and strongly dislikes the disposable fashion culture promoted by certain luxury brands.

She genuinely admires Mara Vell's work, particularly its construction, but considers the prices attached to her collections absurd.

Vera isn't especially revolutionary. She believes people should pay fairly for skilled work, including hers.

She can also be remarkably judgmental about customers' taste.

I'd give her a particular weakness: she sometimes mistakes knowing how to repair something for knowing what someone wants.

A customer may want to discard an old garment for personal reasons, while Vera insists it can be saved.

Her philosophy is simple: things worth making are worth maintaining.

And sometimes she forgets that people are entitled to change.

**A line I'd imagine hearing from her:**

> "That coat's older than you are. Show some respect."

## **02. Hesk — The Bridge Mechanic**

Ghoul · Structural maintenance engineer · Industrial worker · Bellworks connections

**Appearance**

Hesk is a broad, stocky ghoul with gray-brown skin and a heavy, somewhat uneven jaw.

His face is deeply lined, and one side of his mouth never closes quite correctly. His shoulders are powerfully built, while his left leg contains an unmistakably reconstructed section.

He wears a reinforced black industrial coat with shortened sleeves, a thick utility apron, and heavy protective boots.

The coat is covered in old repair stitching, faded safety markings, and the remnants of company insignia.

One shoulder has been patched with an entirely different material. He could afford to replace it, but prefers the repair.

His exposed forearms show practical reinforcement and old damage. Small amber safety lights are mounted on his equipment.

Nothing about him is particularly fashionable.

Which is unfortunate, because he's become fashionable.

**The person beneath the clothes**

Hesk works on the Confluence's railway structures, particularly the older bridges and industrial crossings.

He's competent, experienced, stubborn, and thoroughly unimpressed by people who romanticize industrial labor.

Young Gravepunk enthusiasts occasionally ask where he bought his clothes.

His response is usually to point toward the nearest dangerous maintenance job.

He has a grudging respect for Rook Ravel's political work, but believes the artist sometimes mistakes dramatic gestures for practical improvements.

I'd deepen Hesk by making him a union man who is suspicious of both management and romantic revolutionaries.

He knows which bridges are neglected, which projects are underfunded, and which safety warnings have been ignored.

But he's also contributed to that system by accepting compromises over many years.

His most interesting conflict isn't whether he cares about his fellow workers.

It's how much responsibility he bears for the conditions he's learned to tolerate.

**His response to a luxury Gravepunk jacket:**

> "That's a week's wages. Mine was free. Came with the accident."

## **03. Sil — The Night Courier**

Undead courier · Aspiring racer · Working class · Lower transit districts

**Appearance**

Sil is slender, quick, and slightly awkward when standing still.

Her undead form is relatively humanoid, though her limbs have the narrow proportions and sharp contours typical of the Confluence's more visibly altered residents. We haven't defined her exact undead type.

One side of her head is shaved, while the other carries a sweep of dark, untidy hair.

She wears an oversized black racing jacket with teal panels and faded orange stitching. Several old courier patches are sewn into its shoulders.

Her trousers are fitted for movement, with reinforced knees and deliberately mismatched colored straps.

Her boots are her proudest possessions. She's modified them repeatedly, attempting to imitate the performance footwear associated with Tavi Senn.

A small cyan electronic ornament rests against her throat. It introduces a faint, deliberately crunchy effect when she speaks.

It's not medically necessary.

She just thinks it sounds fantastic.

**The person beneath the clothes**

Sil delivers packages through the Confluence's crowded industrial and commercial districts.

She spends her working hours navigating rooftops, railway passages, narrow maintenance corridors, and busy streets.

She's a passionate admirer of Tavi Senn and Edda Static.

But the admiration means different things.

Tavi represents freedom through movement. Edda represents the possibility of turning something strange about yourself into artistic identity.

Sil wants both.

She is talented, impatient, and frequently overconfident. She dreams of competing professionally, but her opportunities are limited by equipment costs and access to the racing circuits.

I'd give her a slightly uncomfortable habit: she occasionally takes dangerous shortcuts not because they're necessary, but because she wants people to notice how good she is.

That makes her exciting company and an occasionally unreliable employee.

She might be one of the first ordinary Confluence residents to find Cole's movement genuinely impressive.

Not because he's special or destined for greatness.

Because she's spent years practicing something he does almost instinctively.

And she might find that infuriating.

**Her attitude toward racing:**

> "You know what's beautiful about a finish line? It's the only thing that can't tell you to slow down."

## **04. Dorian — The Registry Clerk**

Skeleton · Administrative professional · Aspiring social climber · Central Confluence

**Appearance**

Dorian is a tall, slender skeleton with a carefully polished skull and nearly symmetrical features.

He wears an immaculate charcoal uniform inspired by fashionable formal mourning attire.

His coat has a raised collar, narrow silver piping, and a decorative arrangement of gold-colored rib ornaments imitating the work of Corven Rusk.

His boots are polished. His gloves are spotless. His posture is meticulously controlled.

One glove is almost never removed.

Beneath it, his left wrist contains an older industrial repair that he considers humiliatingly unattractive.

Nothing is mechanically wrong with the repair, as far as we've established. He simply hates the way it looks.

His expensive-looking ornaments are plated imitations, something he hopes nobody recognizes.

**The person beneath the clothes**

Dorian works in the municipal Registry.

His job involves identity records, transfers, applications, and the sort of administrative procedures that make newcomers feel particularly helpless.

He is intelligent, organized, capable, and desperately concerned with appearing more important than he is.

He admires Corven Rusk's aesthetic philosophy because it presents bodily refinement as evidence of superior taste.

Dorian has internalized that message rather thoroughly.

Yet I don't want him to be merely a shallow snob.

He genuinely believes accurate records and consistent procedures protect people.

He has probably seen what happens when documentation goes missing or an official makes an arbitrary exception.

His flaw is that he increasingly confuses respect for the institution with obedience to its hierarchy.

He might help someone who cannot afford private assistance, then become furious when a supervisor discovers he bypassed a procedure.

Dorian wants promotion because he associates rank with dignity.

And perhaps the most uncomfortable thing about him is that he's already perfectly capable of doing meaningful work without it.

**His most characteristic complaint:**

> "The rules exist for a reason. I'm simply asking the reason to make itself available."

## **05. Mina — The Flower Seller**

Spectral resident · Market trader · Memorial florist · Riverside district

**Appearance**

Mina's spectral form is pale and softly luminous, with a slightly indistinct outline.

Her features are gentle but not conventionally ethereal. She has a narrow face, tired eyes, and an expression that can become unexpectedly hard when she's displeased.

She wears layered mourning garments in faded lavender, charcoal, and dusty gray.

Small silver plaques hang from the clothing, some engraved with names or decorative marks.

Flowers are woven through her sleeves and veil.

Some are bright magenta. Others are pale, almost colorless, with unusual geometries native to the underworld.

She wears a narrow belt containing cutting tools, binding wire, labels, and small containers for delicate specimens.

The flowers make her immediately recognizable.

But there's always a certain disorder to the arrangement, as though she's been too busy working to maintain the carefully composed appearance.

**The person beneath the clothes**

Mina operates a flower stall in a riverside market.

Her arrangements are used for domestic decoration, personal celebrations, funerary observances, artistic commissions, and private memorials.

She was influenced by Iona Fen's work, particularly the use of physical objects to represent people and histories that might otherwise be forgotten.

But unlike Iona, Mina is firmly commercial.

She charges for her work.

She negotiates aggressively.

And she refuses to apologize for it.

I'd develop her as someone who understands the emotional value of her products perhaps better than her customers do.

That can make her compassionate.

It can also make her manipulative.

She knows that people often pay more when they're grieving, celebrating, or frightened of forgetting.

She insists she's selling craftsmanship rather than exploiting emotion.

Mostly, she probably believes it.

Her one closely guarded personal detail is a memorial plaque she keeps hidden among her tools.

We haven't decided whose name it carries.

I would leave that unresolved.

**Something she might say to a difficult customer:**

> "The flowers are free to grow. Getting them here is expensive."

## **06. Oren — The Amateur Explorer**

Revenant-like resident · Shop assistant · Aspiring traveler · Outer station district

**Appearance**

Oren is a thin, slightly stooped undead man with a pale, almost mineral complexion.

His face bears faint fracture-like marks, and his eyes have a subdued blue-gray luminosity.

He wears an oversized expedition coat made of heavy, dark blue-gray fabric, with a loosely wrapped funerary scarf and reinforced shoulder panels.

His equipment is elaborate.

A battered camera hangs from his chest. A collection of travel badges decorates his sleeves. Several navigation instruments hang from his belt.

His boots are spectacular.

They are also almost completely unworn.

He owns a beautiful set of weather-resistant gloves that he rarely needs within the city.

His appearance is clearly modeled on the practical expedition aesthetic popularized by Sable Aster.

But unlike Sable, Oren looks as though he has assembled his outfit from books and advertisements rather than experience.

**The person beneath the clothes**

Oren works in a small shop near one of the outer railway stations.

He sells travel supplies, maps, and equipment to people departing into the wider underworld.

He has read nearly everything Sable Aster has published.

He knows obscure route histories, can identify unfamiliar regions from photographs, and has a remarkable memory for geographical descriptions.

But he has never traveled far beyond the Confluence.

He's afraid.

Not necessarily of death, but of the enormous, unpredictable world outside the maintained railway network.

I'd avoid portraying him as a coward.

His fear might be perfectly rational.

He's heard enough stories from returning travelers to understand that the advertisements omit quite a lot.

And he hasn't yet found a reason to accept those dangers.

What makes him interesting is that he still dreams of going.

Every day he helps other people prepare for journeys he cannot convince himself to attempt.

**His favorite phrase:**

> "I've been meaning to see it for myself."

And he means it.

Every single time.

## **What makes these six people different?**

| **Vera**   | Values craftsmanship and preservation; struggles to accept change            |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hesk**   | Values practical responsibility; struggles with the compromises he's made    |
| **Sil**    | Values freedom and recognition; mistakes unnecessary risks for opportunities |
| **Dorian** | Values order and respectability; confuses social status with personal worth  |
| **Mina**   | Values memory and independence; can monetize emotional vulnerability         |
| **Oren**   | Values exploration and knowledge; hasn't reconciled curiosity with fear      |

I like that none of these needs a grand destiny.

Their conflicts are ordinary human problems that have survived death.

And their fashions reflect those conflicts rather than merely indicating membership in a subculture.

Vera values preservation, so her clothing is old but beautifully maintained. Hesk values utility, so he wears genuine repairs without romanticizing them. Sil wants to move beyond her present circumstances, so she dresses like the person she hopes to become. Dorian wants status, and his appearance reveals the lengths he'll go to imitate it.

Mina turns personal symbolism into a livelihood. Oren has built an entire wardrobe around a future he hasn't yet dared to pursue.

**Their clothing is a kind of biography.**

## **How they might appear in the game**

I wouldn't make all six quest-givers.

Some could have actual dialogue. Others might only appear as background inhabitants or environmental details.

Imagine Cole passing through an older commercial street.

Vera is adjusting a mourning veil on a customer. Hesk is examining an overhead support with a look of deep disapproval. Sil nearly collides with someone while making a delivery. Dorian walks past, irritated by the dust on his coat.

Mina is closing her stall, carefully gathering the flowers nobody purchased.

Oren stands outside his shop, watching a departing train.

None of them particularly notices Cole.

He is just another newcomer.

That's an important distinction: their lives shouldn't begin when the player approaches them.

They have places to go, work to do, debts to settle, people to meet, and arguments that existed before Cole arrived.

And if we build their dialogue and environments carefully, players could begin to recognize them without needing formal introductions.

## **What remains unsettled**

Their names, jobs, appearances, celebrity influences, and central personality traits now form a fairly coherent **provisional cast**. But we haven't established their actual ages, former lives, relationships, exact social standing, or the physical requirements of their undead forms.

I deliberately haven't assigned romantic partners, families, political affiliations, or secret tragic backstories to all of them.

Those details should emerge when we have a reason to explore them.

Nor should these six be treated as the definitive demographic makeup of the Confluence. We're still missing ordinary wealthy residents, children or younger arrivals if the setting permits them, people of unusual undead forms, religious practitioners, criminals, and citizens who reject the entire celebrity-driven fashion culture.

### **One thing I particularly want to preserve**

Our celebrities are larger than life.

Our ordinary citizens should not be smaller *as people*.

Mara Vell can influence the fashion of an entire metropolis, but Vera can still have a stronger opinion about the construction of a particular dress.

Tavi Senn can be a legendary racer, but Sil can still experience the thrill of movement in a way that belongs entirely to her.

Sable Aster can explore the unknown, while Oren may be facing a challenge just as significant to him when he finally boards a train.

And perhaps the greatest test of whether we've built a believable society is this:

**Could we write an interesting story about any one of these people without Cole ever appearing?**

I think we're getting close.

My personal favorites to develop further would be **Dorian and Sil**. They represent two beautifully contrasting reactions to the Confluence: one tries to climb its hierarchy, while the other desperately wants the freedom to outrun it.

And they could both be wrong about what freedom actually means. ❤️
